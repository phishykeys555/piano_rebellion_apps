Changelog

Developer changelog for the studio app site (piano_rebellion_apps). For future Claude sessions and maintainers, not for students. Not linked from the hub or any page; noindex.

How to use this file

  • One entry per version bump. Every time an app's version string changes, add an entry here in the same commit.
  • Where the version lives: each app's <folder>/index.html footer, as App Name · Version YYYY-MM-DD.N (for example Modal Metronome · Version 2026-11-20.2). The hub's version is in the root index.html footer. Shared scripts carry their own version in a header comment (for example shared/voice-pitch.js, shared/hype.js).
  • Version format: YYYY-MM-DD.N, where N counts up within the day per app (.1, .2, ...). A hotfix on top of a version may add a third part (2026-11-12.1.1).
  • Order: newest first. App sections are ordered by most recent update; versions inside an app are newest first.
  • Entry format: ### Version YYYY-MM-DD.N, then the headings Added, Changed, Fixed (leave out any heading with nothing under it), then plain-language bullets. Name the folder path of any page or folder added or changed.
  • New app: add a new ## App Name — folder/ section. Say "not on the hub" if the hub does not link it.
  • Privacy: no student or beta tester names (write "beta tester request" or "student feedback"), no emails, no access codes or gate hashes, no payment details. Say "instructor", not the other word. No emojis.
  • Gaps: where a version number was never recorded, the change sits under the nearest known version or under "(version not recorded)". Do not invent numbers.

Version 2026-11-28.2 (beta 2026-11-28)

Drum pad mode (Derek's request): drum students play hits on a practice pad, never held notes. Folder rhythm-trainer/.

Added

  • "Drum pad" answer mode next to Clap or snap, and a direct link rhythm-trainer/?mode=drums that opens straight into it. Graded on attacks only: a half, whole, dotted or tied note is one hit at its start; rests must stay silent; the "Grade how long I hold" option is hidden. Runs on the Clap detector (listens above about 2.8 kHz, so the app's woodblock click is ignored).
  • "Tune the mic to my pad": about a second of quiet (with 4 soft clicks from the speaker) measures the room and the click as the mic hears it, then 8 hits, soft and loud, with a live "Heard it: n of 8" count. The threshold becomes the higher of 2.5x the room/click level and 35% of the softest hit (kept between 0.3x and 4x the default).
  • A 50 ms re-trigger lockout in Drum pad mode (70 ms elsewhere, unchanged): a stick bounce is one hit, while 16ths at 140 BPM (107 ms apart) and fast doubles still count.
  • x noteheads on the staff in Drum pad mode (a circled x for a half or whole note). Drum pad bests are kept apart from Clap bests. "How it works" explains the mode.

Changed

  • Saved settings: two NEW keys only (rt-drum, rt-drumThr). The saved answer mode stays clap while Drum pad is on, so older versions read it as Clap. Mic delay uses the existing "Calibrate timing" (the Clap offset).

  • Separate timing windows for MIDI, tap and microphone; a note held for at least 75% of its written length counts as fully held (lifting a finger between piano notes is not penalized). Instructor feedback.
  • Loop mode leaves whole beats of room after your bar, so the full 1-2-3-4 count-in always shows and plays on the beat. Instructor feedback.
  • The Hear it / Hear + count / Retry / Start buttons are evenly spaced. Instructor feedback.
  • The microphone picker uses the same escaping as Note Sprint's picker (it was already there). Instructor feedback.

Fixed

  • One note no longer counts the app's own click as extra notes: the click stops once your bar starts (unless the new setting is on), and a sound heard within 30 ms of a click is ignored. Instructor feedback.
  • A take with no notes heard says "No notes heard" and scores nothing: it is not counted in tries, averages or high scores. Instructor feedback.
  • The drum click now plays in Single tries (the first try started before the drum samples had loaded). Instructor feedback.

Version 2026-11-27.15 (beta 2026-11-27)

Groove fix, pass 2: the band. Pass 1 corrected the drums and percussion; this pass gives the bass and the two keyboard players the parts the groove studies write for each groove, adds fills and section "gears" by style, and fixes the measured misses from pass 1. Folder drum-machine/, beta page drum-machine-beta/. Nothing was checked by ear.

Added

  • A bass line per groove where a study gives one (Bass style = Auto). Each is a fixed one- or two-bar line placed on that groove's own kick, moved through the chords by chord tone, with an approach note into the next chord: the James Brown type grooves (the One long, a note on each off-beat kick, a pickup into the One), the Cissy Strut type two-bar line, Look-Ka Py Py type, the laid-back pocket, go-go in two bars, Minneapolis octaves, half-time funk, the Not Fade Away type cell, partido alto (the bass plays the kick's melody), dembow, boom bap and neo-soul two-bar loops, the Immigrant Song type gallop, the Levee type riff rhythm, the 5/4 rock two-bar figure, Home at Last, and the new grooves below. Articulations, Bass licks and the 5-string still apply.
  • A keys part per groove where a study gives one (Comping = Auto): Clavinet scratch and chank with a single organ stab for the James Brown type grooves; Clavinet with organ stabs on the kick for the Tower of Power type grooves; organ two-note answers for the New Orleans funk grooves; Motown piano on the quarter notes with the organ taking the chop on 2 and 4; cha-cha-cha, Afro-Cuban 6/8, baião (chords on the upbeats), partido alto, soca and ska upbeat stabs; samba figures cut from the two-bar tamborim phrase; a bossa cell that follows the drummer's own rim phrase (it turns with the Clave menu); stride-style quarter notes for New Orleans two-beat; sparse Basie answers for big band; held fourth-chords for the Elvin groove; anticipations only at fast swing; the Take Five type and 7/4 figures; Chicago shuffle stabs on the skip notes; the half-time shuffle figure; hammered triplets for the Hold the Line type groove; boom bap's two-bar chop; grand piano for the Stash type grooves.
  • Six grooves the studies mark high confidence: Reggae Rockers, Ska, Half-Time Shuffle with the open hat on the skip (rock type), Rock Shuffle, Punk D-Beat, Motown Tom Backbeat (approx.).
  • Fill families: James Brown type grooves get set-ups only (a ghost pickup, one open hat, two snares into the One: no tom runs); Tower of Power type grooves get 16th-note snare set-ups; reggae gets the high tight roll into the drop; the Cuban fills get the timbale abanico; jazz gets the bebop set-up (snare, snare, kick on the last 8th) and a one-bar trading figure; big band gets the crescendo set-up.
  • Gears in the lifted eight bars (or the Chorus, with Song sections on): the blues shuffle bass walks in the verse and doubles the shuffle in the lift; the Texas shuffle goes from its one-bar figure to the two-bar walk; the two-feel bass walks four; the Stash type grooves thicken the piano and drive the last two bars in 8ths; pop alternates two bars of octaves with two bars of 8th-note roots.
  • A third conga voice: in the Cuban two-bar grooves the two low open tones of the 3 side (the bombo and beat 3) are played on the lower drum, and turn with the Clave menu.

Changed

  • Jazz swing against tempo follows the jazz study's curve from records: 2:1 up to 90 BPM, widest (2.3:1) from 100 to 140, back to 2:1 at 200, 1.5:1 at 240 and 1.2:1 at 280. A fixed place for a tempo, never random. Keys on the swung 8th now land with the ride's skip note, and the jazz piano sits a share of the beat behind (5%, with a ceiling) in place of a fixed number of milliseconds; the neo-soul keys lag also scales with tempo.
  • Brazilian lilt: in samba, partido alto and batucada the & and the a of each beat sit early by a fixed share of a 16th (6% and 10% up to 105 BPM, half that to 125, none above); bossa gets half of it. The same in every bar, for the whole band; off on Machine pocket.
  • Jazz balance: at jazz dynamics the Jazz kit's ride sat about 10 dB lower against the bass than the Vintage kit's ride playing the same part, and with the kick feathered (pass 1) the cymbal is the drummer. On the jazz grooves the Jazz kit's ride is up 6 dB, its hi-hat 4 dB and its snare 3.5 dB. The feathered kick is unchanged.
  • Tumbao bass: the note on 4 rings to the next bombo; the slow grooves (Conga Tumbao, Son Montuno) state the root on 1 of the 2 side and the octave on 4 of the 3 side; songo adds the root on 1 and a pickup into the bombo; the invented third on the & of 3 is gone. Bossa and samba bass take the fifth below the root where the range allows, play the pickups soft and short, and anticipate the next chord's root. In a two-bar groove only the last time round leads into the next chord (the first bar used to approach it a bar early).
  • Motown bass starts from the root-with-the-kick lines (the eight-8ths line is no longer the default). Ballad, Pop + Shaker and the breaks follow the kick. Samba, bossa, disco, afrobeat and Motown comping cells were rewritten from the studies.

Fixed

  • Trap: with the bass on, the bass IS the 808 (its sub on the kick, with glides); the 808 drum row no longer sounds under it.
  • Ghost notes too soft to hear (26 to 28 dB under the backbeat on Basic Rock, Jazz-Funk, Home at Last, Rosanna and Black Dog type grooves) are lifted to about 23 dB; the order of strengths is kept.
  • Kick and backbeat timing: a soft ceiling keeps every kick and backbeat stroke within 3.3 ms of its place at any Humanize setting (single strokes reached 5.9 ms).
  • Keys timing scatter is held to 3.3 ms (was 3.8).
  • MP3 export: the level is set from the true peak (the peak between the samples as well), so the encoded file stays under full scale; a busy band reached +0.1 dBTP in testing. Exports with such peaks come out a fraction of a dB quieter.

Not changed

  • No groove key was renamed or removed. No new saved setting. Saved settings, share links and .metronome files load as before.

Version 2026-11-27.14 (beta 2026-11-27)

Groove fix, pass 1: every groove on the menu was checked against its groove study (ten family studies with sources and confidence labels) and fixed, replaced or taken off the menu. Folder drum-machine/, beta page drum-machine-beta/. Nothing was checked by ear.

Added

  • Clave / phrase direction (new menu, shown on grooves that have a direction; saved as clv, default Auto). 2-3 or 3-2 for the Cuban grooves, the two-bar phrase or its mirror for bossa and samba. The clave, the bell, the congas, the cross-stick, the piano montuno and the comping cells all turn round together; the bass tumbao (bombo and ponche in every bar) fits both.
  • Six percussion rows for the recorded instruments that had no row: Timbale High and Low, Agogo High and Low, Tamborim, Pandeiro (the pandeiro plays thumb, fingertips, heel and slap from where the note falls and how strong it is). Used in Salsa and Mambo (low timbale on 4 of the 2 side), Songo (timbale answers), Samba and Batucada (tamborim phrase, pandeiro), Baião (agogo on the upbeats), and in the Cuban fills.
  • 18 grooves the studies mark high confidence: New Orleans Funk 2, Swung Clav Funk, Soul-Funk Pocket, Laid-Back Pocket, Go-Go 2 bars, Oakland Linear Funk, Son Montuno (verse), Mambo, Afro-Cuban Jazz, two Clave Trainers (son and rumba), Partido Alto, Samba Batucada, three Classic Breaks, Jazz Waltz in 1, and the 12/8 verse that follows the 11/8 intro. Names describe the feel; a song is named only as "type".
  • Fill vocabulary by style: 30 fill families (big band set-ups, bebop comping fills, press rolls, timbale and conga fills, the samba virada, Motown pickups, go-go, half-time shuffle, hip-hop's one late snare and more) in place of 7. A fill is picked from the run's seed, then varied the way a player varies a lick (a note left out, one ghosted, one moved to another drum, a kick tucked in), with three dynamic shapes. The same fill never comes twice within 16 bars. Fills still land on 1 with kick and crash as one stroke, and the groove still runs under them.

Changed

  • Funk: the Cissy Strut type groove is the beat three sources agree on (linear hats, double backbeat on 4 and the & of 4, kick after each backbeat); the Dean Town type groove is four on the floor at 109; go-go has its e-of-4 kick pickup, heavier swing and a cowbell line; Minneapolis is two flat levels on the LinnDrum kit; half-time funk no longer puts a ghost note on a kick.
  • Jam band: the 11/8 intro has kick and ride on each group with the snare between (no backbeat); the Stash jam has the kick figure and the bell on the & from Bill Carbone's analysis in place of a guessed kick.
  • Jazz: Jazz Swing and Up-Tempo Swing are four-bar phrases with a feathered kick and light comping (the borrowed performance played the kick at full strength on every beat, so these grooves are several dB quieter in the low end than before, on purpose); two-feel is the hi-hat version with a feathered kick; bebop, big band, broken time and the Tony Williams ride are four-bar phrases (no lick repeats every bar); the Elvin groove has the ride-snare-kick triplet order; the jazz waltz is two bars; the Kansas City hi-hat opens on the skip note; New Orleans two-beat has press rolls and no tambourine. Snare and kick comping on the swung note now move with the ride (they stayed on the exact triplet: a flam at fast tempos).
  • Blues: the blues shuffle and the Texas shuffle are double shuffles (both hands, kick on all four, hat foot on 2 and 4); half-time shuffle and slow blues are two-bar phrases with ghost notes as soft as measured.
  • Afro-Cuban: salsa has a lighter bombo kick, the clave-aligned campana and the two-drum tumbao; songo has the bell on the pulses and timbale answers; cha-cha-cha has its kick on the & of 2 and the hat foot on all four; the conga tumbao groove is a real two-bar son clave groove.
  • Brazilian: bossa has the hat foot on 2 and 4 and quiet feet; samba is the two-bar telecoteco phrase with tamborim and pandeiro; brush bossa is constant soft taps with the phrase as accents.
  • Reggae and world: one drop accents 2 and 4 on a thinner hat and opens it in bars 2 and 4; afrobeat alternates the notated Tony Allen bar with its answer and plays light (no rimshots); maqsum and saidi have their soft filler strokes.
  • Rock and pop: the ride-cymbal rock groove and the swung-8ths groove play as written (their borrowed performance was a 12/8 take); punk, waltz and 6/8 have shaped dynamics; Motown's tambourine plays 8ths with 2 and 4 struck.
  • Hip-hop: boom bap, neo-soul and the Dilla-feel groove are two-bar loops; trap is two bars on the Trap kit, and the 808 follows the chord (it used to hold the key note against the bass line).
  • Songs and odd meters: Take Five has the kick on 1 only and the snare comping; the 7/4 groove has its kick pickup and crashes; the 9/8 grooves play 2+2+2+3 three times and then 3+3+3; Josie plays all four bars; Tom Sawyer's second kick is on the & of 3; Good Times Bad Times has cowbell and hi-hat foot.
  • Keys and bass by groove: organ for the New Orleans funk grooves and go-go, Wurlitzer holds for the four-on-the-floor funk, piano for the Chicago shuffle, electric piano for the half-time shuffle, gospel comping (not jazz) for swung 8ths; in-one bass for the fast waltz.
  • Ghost notes written from the studies sit 20 to 23 dB under the backbeat on this engine's scale.
  • Default tempos were added where the studies give one; a groove that brings a drum-machine kit (Trap, LinnDrum) hands the kit back when you leave it.

Fixed

  • Same settings, same seed, same notes. One function now starts every run (Play, MP3 export, Copy auto to roll). Ghost-note levels, snare rolls, Machine-pocket spread, random mutes, the dropped ghost and "Change it up" drew from the page's random numbers and from state left by the run before; all of it now comes from the saved seed.
  • Deep studio kit: the cross-stick's room signal dropped 1.56 dB between two neighbouring strengths; it is now continuous (largest step 0.6 dB).

Taken off the menu (keys still load)

  • fjb (a second copy of Funky Drummer) plays jb_funky_drummer; gen_follow_you (one source, could not be confirmed) plays popshaker. Saved settings, share links and .metronome files that name them still load.

Added

Studio, Vintage, Jazz and Rock now load rebuilt sets with every usable recorded level of every stroke: up to 16 recorded loudness levels per stroke (the snares have 13 to 15, of which 4 to 9 lie in the ghost-note range) instead of 3 to 6. The Jazz kit keeps its ride from the earlier set (twelve hits against six), re-levelled take by take.
  • Brushes is now a whole kit (drum-machine/kits/brushes-swirly, Swirly Drums by Karoryfer Samples, CC0): snare taps, edge taps and digs, recorded stirs at four strengths and flutters for the sweeps, a brushed ride with twelve levels, hats in seven positions, toms, cymbals and its own bass drum. The older brush set stays as a fallback.
  • New kit: Deep studio (drum-machine/kits/deep-smdrums, SM Drums by Scott McLean, Tod Stillwell and Suleiman Ali): the most detail of any kit here, 15 snare levels with six takes each, 16 closed hi-hat levels, tight, loose, bark and splash hats, three crashes and a splash cymbal. Its permission is the authors’ own “free for any use” statement, not a formal licence file; it is one entry in the kit table and can be taken out by deleting that entry.
  • More ways of striking, all real recordings: hi-hat with the tip or the shank of the stick, quarter-open, half-open and three-quarter-open hats chosen by how hard the note is, the bark (an open hat closed at once) and the sizzle; the full rimshot on the hardest backbeats; a recorded flam in jazz comping and a two-stroke flam on fill accents; off-centre and rim strokes on the jazz toms; shank on the ride bell; the ride crashed on its edge in jazz; a soft crash on the bow of the cymbal; a third crash and a splash cymbal on the Deep studio kit; a choked ride when the band stops; a left-foot kick for fast doubles on the Rock kit.
  • Kick feel and Snare feel pick a real recording where the kit has one: Boomy plays the open, undamped kick (Studio, Vintage), Tight plays the dry kick and the damped snare (Jazz).

Changed

  • Loudness is continuous. Every stroke lands on the exact level its strength asks for, blended between the two nearest recorded levels (the blend allows for how alike the two recordings are, so the level does not bump in the middle). Version 2026-11-27.12 (beta 2026-11-27)

Changed

  • The organ no longer leaves 79 tonewheels turning. A key’s contacts are built when the key goes down and dropped when it comes up, each starting at the phase its wheel would have reached by then — so a note still starts in the middle of a cycle (the key click) and two keys sharing a wheel still line up, but nothing is left running between notes. Before, every key ever played left nine gates and the whole wheel bank in the graph for the rest of the session, so the cost grew all evening and never came back down; that is what made a phone stutter on the organ pairs. Measured on the same four bars of funk, the organ’s own cost is roughly a quarter to a third lower, and it no longer climbs with every new key. Every organ measurement held: drawbar levels within 0.1 dB, A = 440 exactly, the key-click burst and its release click, percussion decay and single trigger, the C3 chorus side lines at ±6.87 Hz, Leslie horn 0.80 Hz slow and 6.65 Hz fast with the spin-up, drum 0.66 / 5.55 Hz, the 800 Hz crossover and the stereo spread.
  • The organ also skips what it is not using: the scanner’s delay line is unhooked when the vibrato is off, a stab too short to show it does not pay for the wheel-leakage tone, and a drawbar sitting 18 dB down is not given a contact on a stab. A cap keeps the number of sounding contacts in hand on nine-drawbar registrations.
  • The tremolo wheel on each keys strip is only built, and only wired up, for a sound that has tremolo. A wheel turning into a depth of zero still cost the audio thread a sample-by-sample pan on every block.
  • A held chord sings on. The recorded pianos stop at about 2.2 s at middle C with a fade on the end, so long ballad chords used to just die. Now, when a chord is held past that, the note carries on with a looped slice of the recording’s own steady tail — a whole number of cycles so the splice lands on the same point of the wave, the step between the two splice points measured rather than guessed, and the decay carrying on at the rate that tail was decaying at. Middle C on the grand used to be silent from 2.5 s; it now decays smoothly past 6 s at about 9 dB a second. No extra download.
  • The tine piano goes through a combo amp. The recording is direct out of the instrument, so on its own it was a dry tine with tremolo and nothing around it. Added cabinet thump, the mid scoop, cone presence, a little power-stage squeeze and the 12-inch roll-off: +2 to +3 dB below 1 kHz, +7 dB at 1–2 kHz, +16 dB at 2–4 kHz, +25 dB at 4–8 kHz. Tremolo is unchanged, and the level was re-trimmed so it still sits within 0.8 dB of the grand at the same settings.
  • The clav never strikes twice the same. The pad meets the string a little differently every time: the first few ms are brighter by a different amount, the tone sits a little high or low, the level moves, and the yarn damper’s cluck comes back at its own pitch and loudness. The jam figure, which used to sit on one two-bar cell for eight bars, now moves on every two bars, and one stab a bar turns into a mute or a rest from that bar’s own seeded draw, so the same figure never comes round twice the same.
  • Pads hold the chord’s colour, not its root. A pad used to fall back on the root and the fifth four times in five; now it reaches for the 3rd and the 7th, a 9th over the 5th, the 13th on a dominant, and keeps root-and-fifth for chords that really are bare power chords. Measured over twenty grooves: bare root-and-fifth pads fell from 81% to 17%.
  • Fills have a vocabulary. Each style keeps its own handful of shapes and they come round in turn, so two fills running are never the same shape: runs, an enclosure round the target note, a chromatic walk-in, double stops in sixths and in thirds, a repeated-note motif, a triplet answer, octave lines, organ smears and the gospel 4-to-3. Over twelve styles and 256 bars the number of different fill shapes played went from 78 to 89, and styles that only ever had one shape (ballad, country, New Orleans) now use four or more.
  • Dominants get style colours. Jazz, gospel, blues, soul and jam players reach for a ♯9, ♭13, ♯11 or ♭9 on a dominant even when the chart just says 7, so they now do here: on a progression with real dominants, jazz went from 22% of dominant hits carrying a colour to 59%, gospel 19% to 49%, slow blues 16% to 61%, neo-soul 18% to 46%. Pop (10%), rock (4%), Motown (1%) and reggae (7%) are unchanged, because those players do not. A chord written with its own tensions is always left alone.
  • When the two players land on the same step, Keys 2 moves instead of vanishing. Up an octave if that clears the top of the other chord, else down to its top two notes, else off the step by a 16th into a hole; dropping the hit is the last resort.
  • The sampler doubles up two velocity layers over a narrower band of the velocity range, and leaves out the damper sound under a whisper — fewer voices for the same chord.
