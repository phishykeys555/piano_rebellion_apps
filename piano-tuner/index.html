    How it works

    Play one key and let it ring. The tuner finds the note on its own, anywhere from A0 to C8, lights that key on the strip and spins the strobe at that note. The outer ring follows the fundamental, the inner rings its 2nd and 4th partials. Turning right means sharp, turning left means flat, standing still means in tune; the In tune light comes on inside the window you pick in Settings (±½, ±1 or ±2 cents).

    Piano tone is mostly overtones and fades fast. The tuner reads the fundamental: in the bass, where the lowest strings barely sound their fundamental, it works it out from the 2nd and 4th partials and corrects for string stiffness. When the key fades out, the last reading stays on screen (dimmed) until you play again.

    It tunes to equal temperament from A4 (440 Hz, or the pitch you set in A4 calibrate), with no stretch. A piano technician stretches the top and bottom octaves on purpose, so a well-tuned piano can read a few cents sharp at the top and flat at the bottom. Tap any key on the strip, or "Hear", to play it on a real grand piano recording.

    Tuning check lists every key you held until it settled, with its reading in cents.

    Keyboard shortcuts

    • Space play the note shown on the "Hear" button
    • ← → pick the key a half step lower or higher and play it
    • ↑ ↓ A4 calibrate up or down 1 Hz
    • 1 2 3 in-tune window ±½, ±1, ±2 cents
    • F switch between sharps and flats
